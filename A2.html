Kaleidoscope Lantern Lamp

A2: Generative Artifact Proposal and Physical Prototypes

Idea

The idea was to make a desk lamp with different layers of translucent paper inside it. Each layer will have a different pattern cut into it made by different generative algorithms and will be a different colour. The lamp is similar to a kaleidoscope. A single light bulb in the middle will shine through all the layers and show the patterns on the outer surface of the lamp. The layers will move slowly on their own, so the patterns keep changing as they overlap. I want to see how different designs and colours look when they come together, and how the lamp can create something new just by moving the layers around.

System

I'll use code to generate the cut-out patterns for each layer instead of drawing everything by hand. The basic structure of the lamp and the central light will stay the same, while the patterns, colours and movement can change.

FixedThe lamp base, central light source, outer cover and overall structure.
RandomThe placement and size of the cut-outs, the colours of the layers and the rotation speed of each layer.
AlgorithmsScatter, Poisson-disc sampling, phyllotaxis (golden spiral), loops and randomness To generate variations in the patterns and their arrangement. Each one only decides where the holes go, so any of them can be swapped into any layer.
TechniquesSeeded randomness, loops, recursion (fractal rings, tree), rejection, rotation, cellular automata, and unrolling a pattern around a cylinder so it wraps without a seam.

References

Sol LeWitt, wall drawings. The artwork is the instruction, and anyone who follows it makes a different but related drawing. That is how a family of layers can come from one written rule.

Yayoi Kusama, Infinity Mirror Rooms. A few lights and mirrors become something that feels endless. I want that feeling of depth from layers of light and colour, without a screen.

Kaleidoscopes. I like how simple shapes and colours create more complex patterns when they overlap or reflect. I'm exploring a similar effect using physical layers that move around a light source.

Form

The final object will be a cylindrical desk lamp, standing on a stable base. A single LED will sit in the centre, surrounded by three or four cylindrical layers made from translucent paper or coloured acetate. Each layer will have a different algorithm-generated cut-out pattern and will rotate slowly using a small motor. The layers will be removable, so I can change their patterns and colours to create different versions of the lamp. A translucent outer cover will help diffuse the light while allowing the patterns to show through. The lamp will also cast coloured shapes onto the desk and nearby walls.

First sketches and prototypes
